1. WHO PAYS THE RAIL — the shipped answer, not the assumed one
==============================================================================
  Deposit leg  StripeService:3059  credited = tx.amount - tx.feeAmount
  Payout leg   StripeService:1723  netPayout = amount - payoutFeeCharged
  => platform variable margin on BOTH money legs is exactly $0.00, and the
     2.9%+$0.30 cannot make it insolvent at any volume. The prior pass's
     "1.777% < 3.15%, the platform loses money at every volume" compares a
     revenue rate against somebody else's cost.

  What the pass-through does NOT cover, because it is priced per ACCOUNT and
  application.yml has no knob for it:
     Stripe Connect  $2.00 per monthly ACTIVE ACCOUNT  (published)
     -> a seller who withdraws once in a month costs the platform $2.00
        REGARDLESS of how little they withdrew. It is the only cost in
        the model that does not shrink with the trade size, and it is the
        one a sub-dollar market cannot carry. Priced in takerate.py.

4. WHERE THE TWO RAILS CROSS — solved, not asserted
==============================================================================
  Crypto beats Stripe on a deposit D when the per-transfer network fee N
  satisfies   1.5%D + N + 0%(D-fee) + N  <  2.9%D + $0.30 + 0.25%C + $0.25

     deposit     break-even N/leg   at 1.0% (floating)   verdict against the published numbers
       $1.00              $0.2750              $0.2800   crypto wins ONLY on a cheap chain (BSC-class, $0.05)
       $2.00              $0.2850              $0.2900   crypto wins ONLY on a cheap chain (BSC-class, $0.05)
       $5.00              $0.3100              $0.3250   crypto wins ONLY on a cheap chain (BSC-class, $0.05)
      $10.00              $0.3550              $0.3800   crypto wins ONLY on a cheap chain (BSC-class, $0.05)
      $20.00              $0.4350              $0.4850   crypto wins ONLY on a cheap chain (BSC-class, $0.05)
      $50.00              $0.6850              $0.8100   crypto wins on an optimised TRC20 hop, not on their default
     $100.00              $1.0950              $1.3450   crypto wins on an optimised TRC20 hop, not on their default
     $500.00              $4.3800              $5.6300   crypto wins even on USDT-TRC20

  Read against NOWPayments' OWN published default of $3.50 per USDT-TRC20
  payout, the crypto rail is DEARER than Stripe on every deposit below
  about $500. The crypto rail only wins on a cheap chain, and their own
  cheap-chain figure ($0.05, BSC) wins at every deposit size in the table.
  So the question is not 'crypto or card' -- it is WHICH CHAIN, and that
  is a term nobody has picked. `rustyroyale.payout.nowpayments.currency`
  has NO DEFAULT for exactly this reason, and the comment in
  NowPaymentsPayoutGateway says so: 'a guess here sends real money over
  the wrong chain'.

5. THE FLOOR — what each rail refuses to move at all
==============================================================================
  Stripe        MIN_DEPOSIT $1.00 (ours, not Stripe's). Round trip 57%.
  NOWPayments   "about $2 for one half of the coins that we support and
                about $3-5 for another" -- and the minimum itself moves
                with the network fee, so the floor rises exactly when the
                rail gets expensive. A crypto rail RAISES the shipped
                minimum deposit from $1.00 to $2.00-$5.00.
  NOWPayments payout minimum: NOT PUBLISHED. The only adjacent published
                figure is "The minimum conversion amount is $1".

  What the crypto rail actually buys, stated exactly: it removes the
  $0.30 + $0.25 FIXED legs and the $2.00/month Connect account fee, and
  replaces them with one network fee per transfer whose size is chosen by
  the chain. On a cheap chain that is a 6-to-30x reduction in the fixed
  cost of moving a small balance. On the chain their own calculator
  defaults to, it is 7x WORSE. The rail is not the decision; the chain is.

  And the cost neither table shows: the seller must hold a crypto wallet
  and an address to be paid to. That is a product barrier, not a fee, and
  it is being asked of somebody cashing out a few dollars of idle-game
  loot.
